HI, I'M Showmik

Modeling and Designing Next-Generation Semiconductor Devices

I am Showmik, a PhD candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Missouri–Columbia, specializing in wide-bandgap and ultra-wide-bandgap semiconductor devices, power electronics, and device reliability. My research focuses on developing and analyzing next-generation semiconductor devices for high-efficiency power conversion and operation in extreme environments, including radiation and elevated temperature.

My technical focus includes semiconductor device physics, TCAD-based device modeling, electrical characterization, and reliability analysis of GaN HEMTs and β-Ga2O3 MOSFETs. I investigate device behavior under heavy-ion radiation, temperature variation, and different operating conditions to better understand transient response, charge-collection mechanisms, and device performance. My simulation workflow includes tools such as Silvaco Atlas and Synopsys Sentaurus, together with MATLAB and Python for data analysis and post-processing.

Beyond conventional device simulation, I integrate machine learning with semiconductor device modeling to accelerate parameter extraction, device optimization, and inverse design. My work includes developing predictive models to extract physical device parameters directly from electrical characteristics and reduce the time required for device design and optimization.

My research experience also extends to semiconductor fabrication, device characterization, compact-model extraction, and interdisciplinary experimental work. I have contributed to projects involving AlN/β-Ga2O3 heterostructure devices and nanostructured biosensor fabrication using two-photon polymerization and thermal processing. In addition to research, I have several years of university-level teaching experience and enjoy mentoring students in semiconductor devices, electronics, circuit analysis, and engineering problem solving.
